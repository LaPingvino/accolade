# Fun Things to Try

Made from Accolade's own list of commands, so nothing is left out. In the app each line is a link that does it: on your script, or on the sample when that suits better.

## Writing

- Open any screenplay PDF and keep writing: Accolade reads it back into a script (try it on the sample's, printed by Lexington) (Import PDF as Screenplay) — **Ctrl+O, or drop it on the window**
- Open the sample, Chekhov's chameleon half adapted, and break whatever you like (Open Sample)
- Start a script in the language you write in: New brings a scene of its own (New) — **Ctrl+N**
- Write in Hebrew, Arabic, Japanese or Esperanto: the cues and headings follow the language (Script Language)
- Begin from a structure, Save the Cat or the hero's journey, and fill in the beats (New from Structure)
- Plan in sections alone: turn on Every Section Is a Beat and each one waits in the Notes pane until a scene grows from it (Every Section Is a Beat)
- Make any line any element with Ctrl+1 to 8; mostly Tab and Enter guess it first (Character) — **Ctrl+3**
- Ask for a new scene and get the heading written for you (New Scene)
- Leave yourself a note mid-line; the Notes pane gathers them all (Add Note) — **Ctrl+Shift+K**
- Drop a marker where you will come back, and hop between them (Marker) — **Ctrl+M**
- Watch the outline grow beside you (Outline) — **Ctrl+Shift+O**
- Lay the scenes out as index cards, or on a timeline with a lane per character (Cards) — **Ctrl+Shift+D**
- Colour the scenes and see the whole story as a strip over the page (Timeline Strip)
- Meet everyone who speaks, and rename one everywhere at once (Characters)
- Put a storyboard frame between the lines and draw it in Inkscape or Krita (Storyboard Frame)
- Pull another file into the script, as Highland does (Include File)
- Fill the title page in as a form (Title Page) — **Ctrl+Shift+T**
- Write the treatment or the novel next door in Markdown, footnotes and citations included (New Markdown Document)
- Let the source suggest the next line while you write; Tab takes it (Suggest from the Source While Writing)
- Go full screen and let the rest of the world fall away (Fullscreen) — **Ctrl+Shift+F, Alt+Enter**
- Write with Vim or Helix keys, if your fingers want them (Editor Keys)
- Forget the menus: a few letters find any command (Command Palette) — **Ctrl+K, F1**
- Show a key on every ribbon button and never reach for the mouse (Ribbon Key Tips) — **F10**
- Try another theme, or larger text for a long night (Theme)

## Rewriting

- Move a scene or a speech up and down without cut and paste (Move Up) — **Alt+Up**
- Fold the script to its sections and see the shape of the story (Fold All (Sections Only))
- Keep the pages open beside you; they follow your typing (Preview PDF) — **Ctrl+Shift+P**
- Fix one line and let Accolade fix every line like it (Fix Lines Like This)
- Save a draft before the big rewrite, and compare any two later (Save Draft)
- See what changed since you opened the script (Changes) — **Ctrl+Shift+H**
- Walk through the open items, to-dos, cuts and notes, one by one (Next Open Item) — **F8**
- Hear it: the table read gives each character a voice (Table Read)
- Write together and follow each other's cursors (Edit Together)
- Count the pages, the scenes, and who talks most (Statistics)

## Adapting

- Choose a story as the source and keep it beside the script (Choose Source)
- Step through the source passage by passage (Next Source Passage) — **Ctrl+Alt+J**
- Turn a passage into a scene draft to rewrite (Adapt Source Passage) — **Ctrl+Alt+A**
- Let Accolade guess which passage a scene came from (Link Guess for This Scene) — **Ctrl+Alt+G**
- Mark a passage as cut, so leaving it out was a choice (Mark Source Passage as Cut)
- See what Accolade learned from the way you adapt (What Accolade Learned)
- Lock the source, and relink only what changed in it (Lock Source)

## Producing

- Switch between writer and production and see only what you need (Working As)
- Lock the scene numbers, add a scene, and watch it become 12A (Lock Scene Numbers)
- Issue a draft in its colour; the next changes get their stars (Issue Draft)
- Lock the pages and print only the revised ones (Lock Pages)
- Tag the props, the cast and the wardrobe right in the text (Tag Selection) — **Ctrl+Shift+B**
- Break the script down scene by scene (Break Down)
- Shuffle the shoot on a stripboard (Stripboard)
- Plan the shoot days and send them to your calendar (Schedule)
- Print the reports a first AD asks for (Reports)

## Exporting

- Export to PDF, Final Draft, Word, HTML or an e-book (Export) — **Ctrl+E**
- Print it, title page and all (Print) — **Ctrl+P**
- Export the writer's version and the shooting version from one script (Export Writer's Version)
- Hand over a clean Fountain, without the notes and marks (Export Clean Fountain)
- Turn the synopses into a treatment, ready to send (Export Treatment (PDF, Word))
- Take the outline to Workflowy or OmniOutliner (Export Outline (OPML) — for Workflowy, OmniOutliner)
- Give an editor the storyboard's timing as OTIO (Export Storyboard Timeline (OTIO))
- Keep a script's Lexington settings as your own (Add to My Settings)

The command palette (**Ctrl+K, F1**) finds every one of these by name, and the [Keyboard Shortcuts](shortcuts.md) list every key.
