# Drafts, Compare and Merging

Accolade keeps earlier versions of the script with it, as drafts, so that you can go back, see what changed, and take in changes someone made to a copy elsewhere.

## Drafts

- **Collaboration > Save Draft…** keeps the script as it is now, under a name. A text the same as one kept already is not stored twice.
- **Collaboration > Drafts…** lists them: Compare one with the script now, or Restore it (as a new draft; the script as it was is kept first, nothing is overwritten).
- In an .accolade they live inside the file; for a plain .fountain, Accolade keeps them beside it.

## Compare

**Collaboration > Compare…** opens the Compare middle tab: From and To, any two drafts, the script now, or a file (**File…**). The changed scenes are shown with their changed elements, word by word: side by side on a wide screen, one under the other on a phone. Next and Previous Change go round the changed scenes. Comparing only reads: nothing is written.

## Merge Changes

When a copy of the script was edited outside Accolade — a co-writer in Highland or Final Draft, production's FDX, a Clean Fountain someone worked on — **File > Merge Changes…** takes its changes in.

1. Choose the copy: a .fountain, .highland, .textbundle, .accolade or .fdx (a PDF not yet).
2. Accolade finds the version it was made from among the drafts: the one with most of the copy's unchanged parts (the **Base**; change it if it guessed wrong). With no drafts the base is the script now, and every difference counts as theirs.
3. Compare shows only **their** changes since the base. Your own changes since then stay as they are and are not offered. Each change says what it is — they changed, cut or added an element, cut, added or moved a scene — and has **Take** and **Leave**; every change starts as Leave.
4. A **conflict** is a place you both changed: yours and theirs are shown both ways. Take theirs, Leave yours, or **Edit** to write it as it should be.
5. **Take All From This Scene** takes every change of a scene. On the ribbon's Compare tab (and in the Compare menu), Take, Leave and Take Scene act on the change in view and go on to the next.
6. **Merge Taken Changes** writes what you took, and only that, in one step that Undo takes back. The script as it was is kept as a draft first ("Before merging …"), and the result as a new one ("Merged from …"). What you left is still offered, to take later.

What the copy could not carry is not counted as their change: a Final Draft file has no sections, synopses or boneyard; a Clean Fountain has no links to the source. Those stay as they are in your script. A change you take keeps your source links and the locked scene number on its heading; Accolade's blocks at the end (Source, Adaptation with its cut marks, Production, Characters, Notes) are always yours; breakdown tags and the schedule's strips follow their words and scenes.

Merging never writes into a stored draft or an issued revision: each merge is a new draft on top.
