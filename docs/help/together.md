# Writing Together

Two or more people can edit one script at the same time, each in Accolade or in the web version, on any computer or phone. Nobody needs an account, and there is no server that keeps the script: the windows talk to each other directly.

## Sharing and joining

- **Share:** Collaboration > Edit Together… makes a link. Send it to whoever you want to write with (a chat, a mail). Whoever has the link can read and edit the script, so send it only to them.
- **Join:** open the link in a browser (the web version joins by itself), or in Accolade choose Collaboration > Join a Shared Script… and paste the link or its key. Your window takes the shared script; your own script stays where it was.
- **Finding each other:** the windows find each other through public trackers, which only see a number made from the link, never the script; everything sent between the windows is locked with the link's key.
- **The status bar** says the script is shared and how many others are connected. If someone runs another version of Accolade, it says whose is older: that one needs updating before you can write together.

## What is shared, and what stays yours

| Shared | Yours only |
|---|---|
| The script's text, as you type | Your undo history |
| Its language, format and document type (screenplay or Markdown) | Where you save it, and its file name |
| The source, when it is kept beside the script, in a bundle or as a kept copy | Your drafts and compares |
| Characters, notes, learned notes and the source block, which are part of the script's text | Your settings and the look of your window |
| The breakdown's tags and the shooting schedule (days and strips) | The stripboard's Undo, which takes back only your own last move |
| Who is here and where their cursor is | |

Each of you saves your own copy, where you choose. A script you joined is kept by Accolade for that link, so a reload or a later visit with the same link starts from it, even when nobody else is online, and merges with the others' when they come back.

## Seeing each other

- **Who is here:** while the script is shared, the ribbon has a **Session** tab (a Session menu in the Menus look): everyone else who is here, each in a colour of their own.
- **Their place in the script:** a short bar in their colour in the margin left of the text marks the line their cursor is on; what they have selected is tinted in their colour. Point at the bar (or tap it on a phone) to see whose it is. None of it is in your way: the bar is in the margin, and tapping it leaves your cursor where it is.
- **In the outline and on the cards:** a dot in their colour on the scene they are in.
- **Follow:** tap someone on the Session tab (or Session > Follow) and your cursor goes to their line each time theirs moves, until you type, they leave, or you choose Stop Following.

## Undo

Undo takes back only your own changes, never a co-writer's, and at the right place even when they have written above it since. A change of yours that someone else has since written into cannot be undone exactly, so it leaves your undo history (with the steps before it).

## Automatic changes

Some changes Accolade makes by itself: the scene number of a new scene in a locked script, and what it learns from a new heading or cue (Adaptation). While shared, only the person who wrote that heading or cue makes them, so nothing is written twice. Two people adding a scene at the same place at the same moment may both get the same A-number; when their changes meet, the one that comes later in the script is numbered again (12A and 12B).

## The source

When the script's source is kept outside the script (beside it, in its bundle or as a kept copy), it is shared too: editing it in the Source pane or as a middle tab reaches the others as you type. A co-writer who has no source of their own keeps the shared one as a copy for that link. If two people choose a different source at the same moment, the later choice is the shared one; the other person is told so on the status line, and can choose theirs again.

## Production together

Tagging and untagging words (the breakdown) and the stripboard's days and strips are shared as they change: two people tagging different words, or moving different strips, keep both; a strip moved by both at once ends where the last move put it, never lost or doubled. A shoot day taken away sends its strips back to the pool for everyone. Each of you keeps the breakdown and the schedule beside your own copy of the script.

## Leaving

Session > Leave (or Collaboration > Edit Together… > Stop sharing) leaves the shared script: it stays in your window as it is, and goes on with the others. Opening, creating or importing another script in a shared window leaves first, so the others keep theirs. In the web version, a hidden tab lets go of the others after two minutes and finds them again when you come back.
