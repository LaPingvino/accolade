# Accolade Help

Accolade is a screenplay editor that writes plain Fountain. These pages are in the app (Help menu, the Help tab of the side panes) and online, one address per page, to share a link.

## Menus, ribbon and palette

- **Menus:** File (new, open, import, save, export, print), Edit (undo, the clipboard, find, Fix Lines Like This, moving lines, the title page, the script's language, Preferences), Format, Adaptation and Production (a screenplay's), Collaboration (writing together, drafts and comparing them), View and Help.
- **Ribbon:** Preferences > General > Look: Ribbon puts tabs of large buttons in the toolbar's place — File, Write, Edit, Adaptation (once the script has a source, or for a story to start a screenplay from), Production (its first group is the stage the script is at: Lock, Revise, Break down or Schedule), Collaboration and View. The ribbon takes the menu bar's place (a Mac keeps its own menu bar): the ☰ button at the end of the tab row opens the same menus, and every shortcut works as before. F10, or Alt pressed and let go alone (not on a Mac), shows a letter on each tab and button (☰ too) to press it from the keyboard. On a phone the buttons are one row that scrolls sideways.
- **Quick tab, Smart and Fixed:** the lock on the ribbon's tab row (beside ☰; also View > Lock Ribbon Order, and its key tip) switches the window's ribbon between two orders in one tap. **Smart** (where a phone starts) puts a **Quick** tab first and in front: the 8 to 12 most useful commands from every tab, so the common ones are there without going from tab to tab (on a phone in two rows, all in sight, that tab a row taller than the others) — for a screenplay the elements, Undo, Save, Find, Preview, and Next Passage and Adapt while adapting (Tag and the Production pane for production); for a Markdown document the headings, Bold, Italic, lists, links and notes — and the other tabs' groups come most used first. **Fixed** (where the desktop starts; the lock shut) is the standard order, as this page describes it. Use counts too: each command given (by a button, a menu, a key or the palette) counts, and the counts halve every two weeks. They are kept on this device only (ribbon-usage.json beside the settings), never in a script, never shared. Nothing moves while you work: the order is made once when Accolade starts; what comes in later (a source chosen, a pin) goes at the Quick tab's end, so switching Smart and Fixed back and forth shows the same two ribbons. Press and hold any ribbon button (or right-click it) to **Pin to Quick**, **Unpin from Quick**, or **Remove from Quick** one it chose itself (it stays off). On the desktop the same commands can also be a row under the ribbon while the order is Fixed, like Office's Quick Access toolbar: View > Quick Row, Preferences > General > Quick row under the ribbon, or Show the Quick Row on a button's right-click menu (off at first).
- **The top row:** the row always in sight — the ribbon's tab row (the script's name, the search field, the lock and ☰ after the tabs), or the toolbar in the Menus look — is yours to set, like Word's Quick Access Toolbar. Right-click any ribbon button (or press and hold it) and choose **Add to the Top Row**; right-click one on the top row for **Remove from the Top Row** (☰ stays: the menus are there). Any command at all, a menu's too: right-click it in the command palette, or View > Add to the Top Row… and pick it. View > Reset the Top Row goes back to the standard row. A phone and the desktop keep their own top rows, as they keep their own lock.
- **The search field:** typing in the top row's search field (Search commands) opens the command palette with what you typed, and the rest goes on there; it also finds a command by its name on the `:` line (`:fix-lines`). On a phone it is the palette's 🔍 button.
- **The script's name on a phone:** the name keeps its place on the top row; the icons used least go into the … menu first, the name only when nothing else can go. Then the … menu has **Rename…** with the name, and so has the Script middle tab (tap it while it is in front, or right-click it).
- **Contextual tabs:** some ribbon tabs are there only while their work is. **Preview** while pages are shown (it comes to the front). **Fix-Up** after importing a PDF or a Final Draft file, until the script is saved or you choose Done Fixing: Fix Lines Like This…, Apply the Kept Rules Again…, and for a PDF Read with OCR Again… (its scans and its garbled pages, the language asked first) and Check for Garbled Text; it comes to the front with the import ([Fixing an Imported Script](fixing-imports.md)). A Word document imports as a Markdown document, which has no Fix-Up tab. **Title Page** while the cursor is in the title page: a key to add — Title, Credit, Author, Source, Draft date, Contact, Notes, Copyright, in the script's language's own words where Lexington has them (العنوان in Arabic) — after the field the cursor is on, the cursor then where its value goes (a key there already: the cursor goes to it); Title Page… (the dialog) and To the Script. It does not push itself to the front (a new script's cursor starts in its title page): it is there beside the others. **Characters** while the Characters pane is in sight (it comes to the front): on the character you tapped or opened in the pane — Rename Everywhere…, Merge Into…, Add Alias…, Not a Character, Their Note (the character opened, their note to type in), Previous and Next Cue, Their Scenes (a scene to go to) and Close; in a Markdown document Rename, Their Note and the places (its character list is a comment before its notes, `<!-- Characters` … `-->`). **Session** while the script is shared (beside the others; the Collaboration tab stays as it is): who else is here, each in a colour of their own (tap one to follow them, again to stop), Follow and Stop Following — following, your cursor goes to their line each time theirs moves, until you type, they leave or you stop — Copy the Link, and Leave (the script stays here as it is, and goes on with the others). In the Menus look these are a Session menu. **Table Read** while the Table Read is the middle tab in front (in the browser, on a phone; it comes to the front): Previous and Next scene, Pick, Narrator, Storyboard, Tints, Follow and Copy the Follow Link, Full Screen, Close ([The Table Read](table-read.md)). **Compare** while Compare is the middle tab in front (it comes to the front): Next and Previous Change (round the changed scenes), Side by Side, Close; while merging (File > Merge Changes…) Take, Leave, Take Scene and Merge too ([Drafts, Compare and Merging](merging.md)); in the Menus look a Compare menu and the bar over the compare.
- **Writer or Production:** Preferences > General > I am working as, or View > Working As. Writer (where new users start) puts production away: the Production menu, the Production side pane and ribbon tab, the toolbar's Production button, the tagging in the right-click menu, and the production settings in Preferences (scene-number lettering, revision marks, the revised page header, CONTINUEDs). The script stays as it is: locked scene numbers stay locked, revision marks print as set, tags and shoot days are kept, and a locked script says so on the status line ("This script is locked for production (Blue) — switch to Production to change that"). View > Working As > Only for This Script keeps a script in its own view whatever the preferences say (a producer can keep one script in Writer). The command palette still finds Production's commands by name, marked "(Production)"; choosing one switches that script to Production. Accolade from before this choice starts in Production if you used production before (a locked or issued recent script, a production setting changed), else in Writer.
- **Side panes:** Outline, Notes, Characters, Cards, Source, Production, Help and Changes are tabs in one dock beside the script, with two slots, one above the other. Each pane's button (toolbar, ribbon View > Panes, Changes on Collaboration; the View menu, the palette) brings it in, brings it to the front if it is behind another tab, and takes it out when it is in sight; View > Side Panes takes them all out and brings them back. A slot alone takes the full height; with nothing shown the dock is gone. To move a tab to the other slot, right-click it (or press and hold, or Shift+F10 on it) and choose Move to Top or Move to Bottom, or use View > Move Pane to Top/Bottom on the pane you picked last. Where each tab goes, the dock's width and the split between the slots are remembered. From the keyboard: F6 goes from the script to each slot's pane in turn; Tab reaches the tab in front of each row, the arrow keys go along the row, Down or Enter goes into the pane, Esc back to the script. On a phone the panes are a sheet under the script, their tabs in one row: those that do not fit are in the More (…) menu at its end, and the tab in front is always on the row; the arrow button makes the sheet taller.
- **Middle tabs:** the middle of the window is a row of tabs too, the script always the first. View > Cards (Shift+D) opens the cards as a tab there; any side pane goes there with its tab's menu, **Open in the Middle** (or View > Open Pane in the Middle): the source and the notes as documents of their own to write in, with Markdown's commands and their own Undo, any other pane as it is. Several are open at once, one in front; tap a tab, or press Ctrl+PgDn and Ctrl+PgUp (View > Next and Previous Middle Tab) to go round them, the arrow keys on a tab go along the row. A middle tab's menu (right-click, press and hold, or Shift+F10 on it) has **Back to the Side** and **Close**; the × at the row's end closes the tab in front. The menus, the ribbon, the status bar and the preview follow the tab in front: the script's pages beside the script, a document's Live Preview beside it, none beside the cards. Adaptation > Source in the Middle brings the source's tab to the front, and again the script's. On a phone the tabs that do not fit are in the More (…) menu at the row's end.
- **Cards and the timeline:** with the cards in front, the ribbon's **Cards** tab comes forward (in the Menus look: a Cards menu, and the buttons in the cards' header). The current card is the scene your cursor is in, so everything agrees with the script. **Rearrange:** Move…, Swap…, Move Up/Down (Alt+Up/Down), Promote/Demote (Alt+Left/Right) — each one undo step, the cursor going with the scene. **Arrange:** Grid or **Timeline**, kept per script: the scenes left to right in story order (top to bottom on a phone) as blocks as long as the scene (in eighths of a page, as the stripboard measures it once the pages are laid out; counted from the lines until then), in their colours, the acts and sections as bands above; an empty scene is an outline, one with only its synopsis an outline with the synopsis in it. **Lanes by Character** gives everyone who speaks a lane (a mark where they speak, a thin line where they are gone); **Lanes by Tag** one for each tag on the headings (`[[London]]`). Tap a block to open the scene; Left/Right go from scene to scene, Enter opens, Alt+Left/Right (Alt+Up/Down on a phone) or dragging moves the scene; Ctrl+wheel, + and - zoom. **Group** (the grid): By Section, By Color, By Character, By Tag — a view only, the script stays as it is (dragging is off while grouped) until **Make It the Order** puts the scenes in that order inside each section, as one undo step. **Card:** New Scene… (after the current card), Color, Fold Synopses (smaller cards: heading and how it starts), Open in the Script; Back to the Script closes.
- **Timeline strip:** View > Timeline Strip (off at first) puts a thin strip of the whole story over the script while you write: each scene as long as it is, in its colour, the acts' starts marked, the scene you are in lit; under it where that is (scene, length, act), whether the scene is still empty or only a synopsis, and who has been gone longest (in scenes). Tap a block to go there; the arrow at its start folds it to a line of colour.
- **Characters:** View > Characters lists who speaks, the most present first: their scenes, speeches and words, where they start, the other names the learned notes have for them, and as Production their cast number. A filter box finds one. Tap a character to go to their next cue (again for the one after); › opens them: their scenes to go to, Next Cue, a note and the fields Age, Look, Arc (and Actor as Production), kept in the script's `/* Characters */` block as you type. Rename Everywhere… renames them wherever the name stands in capitals or as a name (ANNA → HANNA, Anna → Hanna; "will" stays when WILL is renamed), the list, the learned notes and the breakdown's tags with it, in one undo step, the title page and source links left as they are; it offers the notes and the source as well, each with how often the name stands in it, unticked (with the source, the source links' quotes and the adaptation block's cut entries follow, so they still find their passages); Merge Into… makes one character's cues another's and puts their notes together (an Undo after the breakdown or schedule changed says that the cast stays merged there); Add Alias… and Not a Character go to the learned notes. Names in the list that do not speak yet are listed too, and Insert Character offers them. In a Markdown document the pane lists the names the text reads as people, each tapped to where it next stands, and opened has the note and the fields too, kept in a comment before the document's notes (`<!-- Characters` … `-->`, which no Markdown app shows; View > Document Type carries it over).
- **The sample:** File > Open Sample > A Chameleon (on the ribbon: File > New > Sample) opens a script to try everything on: Chekhov's "A Chameleon" (Constance Garnett's translation, public domain in the USA), partly adapted, with its story kept inside as the source, links and a cut passage, learned notes and a character list, locked scene numbers with an OMITTED scene and an A-scene, a writer's draft and the White and Blue issues, locked pages with an A-page, breakdown tags (one lost) and two shoot days. It opens as a copy of its own: change it freely, and Open Sample again gives a fresh one beside it.
- **Samples in other languages:** File > Open Sample also lists a short scene in each language with one, written in it with its own scene words, cues, extensions, transitions and title page keys: Esperanto (the first Universala Kongreso, Bulonjo-ĉe-Maro 1905), German (the Bremen Town Musicians), French (the crow and the fox), Italian (Pinocchio), Dutch (Rembrandt painting the Night Watch), Russian (Krylov's Quartet), toki pona (the ant and the grasshopper), Chinese (守株待兔), Japanese (桃太郎), Hindi (the monkey and the crocodile, from the Panchatantra: English Fountain's words with Hindi in Latin letters, and its variants in Hindi's own words in Latin letters and in Devanagari; Devanagari is not yet shaped in the editor nor printed by the PDF), and right to left Hebrew (the tower of Babel, Genesis 11:1–4, pointed) and Arabic (Ali Baba's "Open Sesame", افتح يا سمسم, vowelled), to try the points, the joining, the page view and the PDF on. They are machine-written, to be checked by a native reader. New starts from the one in the language for new scripts (File > New in:).
- **New in another language:** New starts in the language picked last (File > New in:, or Edit > Script Language… for this window), with its sample, or for a language without one the template in that language's words — its scene word and time, the cue extension, the closing transition, the title page keys — the prose left in English to write over. A language's variants (Hindi in Latin letters, `hi-Latn`, and in Devanagari, `hi-Deva`, beside Hindi) are under More variants… in the language pickers; a file named so (`film.hi-Deva.fountain`) opens in its variant.
- **Command palette:** Ctrl+K or F1 (Help > Command Palette…, or the keyboard button on the toolbar): type part of any command's name, Enter runs it. It shows each command's shortcut and where it lives.

## Using the help

- **In the app:** Help > a page opens it in the Help tab. The picker at the top goes to any page or cheat sheet; the search box finds words in all of them. Links go to the page or heading they name.
- **Online:** Help > Online Help opens the page shown at its web address, for this version of Accolade.
- **Ideas and bugs:** Help > Send Feedback… opens a new issue on GitHub in your browser, with Accolade's version filled in. Nothing is sent until you send it there.
